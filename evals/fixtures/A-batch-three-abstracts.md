**1. Coastal Distance, Environmental Regulation, and Foreign Investment in Chinese Cities**
Do stricter environmental regulations deter foreign direct investment? We study 280 Chinese prefecture-level cities from 2005 to 2018. Because regulation stringency is endogenous to investment, we instrument it with each city's distance to the coast interacted with national ventilation-based pollution targets. Two-stage least squares estimates show that a one-standard-deviation increase in stringency reduces FDI inflows by 12% on average across all cities, overturning the "pollution haven" scepticism in prior work.

**2. GraphDiff: A Neural Method for Detecting Policy Diffusion in Networks**
We introduce GraphDiff, a graph neural network that detects diffusion pathways from adoption sequences. On a synthetic benchmark of 500 simulated networks, GraphDiff recovers true diffusion edges with an F1 of 0.91, outperforming a naive baseline that assumes adoption follows geographic adjacency. GraphDiff generalises to any diffusion setting.

**3. Who Talks to Whom? Climate Policy Networks Among Chinese Provincial Agencies**
Using 6,200 inter-agency documents from 31 provinces (2012–2020), we construct a directed network of policy coordination between environmental, energy and development agencies. Exponential random graph models show that coordination ties are strongly reciprocal and that agencies in provinces with emissions-trading pilots form more cross-sector ties. We discuss implications for climate governance.
